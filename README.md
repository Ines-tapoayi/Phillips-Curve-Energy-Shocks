# Phillips-Curve-Energy-Shocks
## Overview
This project analyzes how energy dependence affects the inflation-unemployment relationship in the Eurozone through an extended Phillips Curve framework.

The study compares:
- France
- Italy

over the 1998–2025 period.

## Objective
Evaluate whether energy dependence modifies the traditional Phillips Curve relationship between:
- inflation
- unemployment

especially during major energy shocks.

## Methodology
Two models are estimated:

### Model 1
Standard augmented Phillips Curve:
- inflation expectations
- unemployment

### Model 2
Extended Phillips Curve including:
- oil price shocks
- supply-side effects

## Econometric Tests
- ADF stationarity tests
- Durbin-Watson autocorrelation tests
- Breusch-Pagan heteroskedasticity tests
- Jarque-Bera normality tests

## Key Findings
### France
- Traditional Phillips relationship remains significant
- Energy shocks improve model explanatory power
- Inflation still partly linked to labor market conditions

### Italy
- Phillips Curve relationship is weaker
- Inflation is strongly driven by energy shocks
- Greater inflation volatility due to higher energy dependence

## Main Conclusion
Energy dependence significantly alters inflation dynamics and weakens the traditional Phillips Curve relationship in highly energy-dependent economies.
